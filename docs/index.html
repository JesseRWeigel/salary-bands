What employers actually filed they would pay

A US employer sponsoring an H-1B must file the wage it is offering, by job title and worksite, and the Department of Labor publishes every filing. It is a large sample of real compensation with none of the self-report bias of a salary aggregator, because a company that files a number is legally committing to it.

171,617 filings kept from 201,700 read, decided between 2025-07-01 and 2025-12-30.

The control that makes the employer table meaningful

Employer medians differ. The question is whether they differ because employers pay differently, or because a table of 836 groups with uneven sample sizes will always show a spread.

So the employer labels were shuffled at random and the whole table recomputed. If the spread survived the shuffle it would be an artefact of the grouping and nothing else.

Real spread of employer medians (p10 to p90): $100,669

Shuffled: $23,217, a factor of 4.3 smaller (seed 20260801).

The spread collapses, so employer identity is carrying real signal and the table above is about who pays what rather than about how the groups were cut.

What this number is not

It is a floor, not an offer. The field used is WAGE_RATE_OF_PAY_FROM, the bottom of the filed range and the figure the employer commits to. Actual offers are routinely higher, and the median filed range top is 1.22x the bottom. Every figure here understates what was paid.

It is not total compensation. Equity and bonus are not in this data at all, which matters most at exactly the companies whose medians look highest.

It is sponsored roles only. These are filings for foreign workers, which is a particular slice of hiring and not a random sample of a company's engineers.
